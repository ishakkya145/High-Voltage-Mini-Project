00 — The problem

One curve, three overlapping causes.

Power transformer insulation is made of cellulose — oil, paper, and pressboard. Its condition governs how long the transformer lasts. Moisture is the main enemy: it lowers dielectric strength and accelerates ageing, often invisibly, until failure.

Frequency Domain Spectroscopy (FDS) is the standard non-destructive way to check insulation condition. It measures the dielectric loss tangent, tanδ, across a wide frequency sweep — roughly 0.1 mHz to 1 kHz. The trouble is that tanδ doesn't respond to moisture alone. Temperature and oil conductivity shift the same curve in similar ways, so reading the plot by eye is unreliable.

tanδ = F(MC, T, σoil, f) moisture content · temperature · oil conductivity · frequency, entangled

This project's approach: measure the entangling clearly first, then let a classifier learn the multivariate pattern across all 22 frequency points rather than reading any single feature in isolation.

02 — Correlation & PCA

22 features, mostly saying the same thing.

Adjacent frequency points on an FDS curve move together — the loss tangent varies smoothly. A Pearson correlation check across all 231 feature pairs confirmed it: mean adjacent-feature correlation of 0.9698, with 43 pairs correlated above 0.95.

That redundancy is the case for dimensionality reduction. After a log₁₀ transform (loss tangent spans several decades) and standardisation, PCA collapses the 22 features down dramatically:

  • PC₁ 60.51% of variance
  • PC₁+PC₂ 91.04% of variance
  • +PC₃ 97.90% of variance

03 — Classification

From a 2D projection to a tuned classifier.

70 samples (23 Dry, 23 Intermediate, 24 Wet), split 80/20 with stratification — 56 training, 14 held-out test. Everything log-transformed and standardised on training statistics only, to avoid leakage.

The first classifier used only the two leading principal components — useful for visualisation, matching the approach of Wijethunga et al. (CEIDP 2021). A linear SVM on PC₁/PC₂ alone reached 85.71% test accuracy, with the confusion concentrated exactly where you'd expect: Intermediate samples bleeding into Wet.

Retaining all 22 standardised features and tuning the SVM (linear kernel, C=100, 5-fold CV) closed that gap: 92.86% test accuracy, 13 of 14 correct. A basic neural network (22→16→8→3) matched the 2PC baseline but not the tuned SVM — with only 70 samples, there isn't enough data for a network to earn its extra capacity. Augmenting the training set with SMOTE (56→280 synthetic samples) closed that gap too, bringing the network to the same 92.86%.
